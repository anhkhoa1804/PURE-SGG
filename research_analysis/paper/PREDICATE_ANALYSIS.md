# Predicate Analysis

## Question

Is the corrected R2 predictive gain concentrated in head predicates? The saved table supports only descriptive support ordering; no predeclared head/middle/tail thresholds exist. I therefore do not create bins or claim a support interaction.

## Population and metric

Both contrasts use the same CAL-CHECK set: 250 images / 3,142 relation rows. Support is the predicate's one-vs-rest positive count. Recall and precision are one-vs-rest argmax diagnostics, not independent per-class experiments. The full source is `research_analysis/R2_per_predicate_effects.csv`; aggregate results and image-cluster interval are in `research_analysis/R2_nested_nuisance_analysis.md` §8. All values are bounded pilot/exploratory.

For the primary G+O contrast, higher support classes show mixed changes: `on` (+0.001779 recall), `has` (+0.009569), `in` (−0.014851), `wearing` (+0.015504), and `of` (−0.004016). The effect is not uniformly positive among the largest classes. Lower-support estimates also vary and are noisier; examples such as `sitting on` have only 15 rows. Because no grouping was registered, these examples are not a post-hoc head/tail test.

Aggregate G+O accuracy rises from 0.667091 to 0.668364 while macro recall falls from 0.137086 to 0.135990. Thus row-weighted argmax behavior and equal-class recall move in different directions. The O contrast has the same broad aggregate pattern: accuracy rises, macro recall falls. The saved per-predicate CSV contains one-vs-rest precision, not a per-predicate accuracy field; no classwise accuracy is invented here. The per-predicate values below are reproduced directly from the saved CSV; no model was refit.

## Conclusion

The saved evidence does not show a uniform predicate improvement and does not establish that the predictive log-loss gain is concentrated in head predicates. The largest classes dominate the row-weighted population, but class-level recall/precision changes have mixed signs. A confirmatory head/tail conclusion is NOT ESTABLISHED.
